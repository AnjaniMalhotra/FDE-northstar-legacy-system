Finance & Compliance · v1.0, effective Jan 2026
🚛 Shipments (TMS) 🕒 Dock Events (DockTrak) 🏷️ Carrier Master 🛡️ TransCheck 📄 Policy Reference

The four documents Finance & Compliance uses to decide whether a carrier invoice is legitimate, what tier of review it needs, and when a carrier's own pattern of behavior — not just one invoice — becomes a risk worth escalating.

1. Accessorial Charge Audit & Dispute Policy

Agreed rates, free-time allowances, and per-hour detention rates for each load are set at booking time and live in the TMS. This policy governs how a detention charge gets checked against those terms — not what the terms are.

2Detention Charge Verification

  • A detention charge is a verified discrepancy only when the billed time differs from the actual dock dwell time (arrival to departure) by more than 15 minutes, and the resulting dollar difference is more than $25. Both conditions must be true — either one alone is not enough.
  • Only an overcharge (billed > actual) can be put on hold as a dispute. An undercharge is a data-quality note, never a hold — flagging routine noise trains people to ignore the system.
  • Worked example: dock log shows arrival 9:00 AM, departure 11:24 AM — 2 hrs 24 min dwell. Free time is 2 hrs, so the real billable overage is 24 minutes: 0.4 hrs × $65/hr = $26.00 actual. If the carrier billed 0.55 hrs (33 min) instead, that's a 9-minute, $9.75 gap — under both thresholds, so it is not a verified discrepancy, just rounding noise. If they'd billed 2.0 hrs instead, that's a 1 hr 36 min, $104.00 gap — over both thresholds, and is verified.

3Escalation Tiers

  • Tier 1 (Analyst-level): a single invoice with a verified discrepancy under $250 may be logged and flagged for carrier follow-up without manager sign-off. This is a flag, not a payment action.
  • Tier 2 (Manager-level), required when any of these is true: the verified discrepancy is $250 or more; the carrier already has 2+ upheld disputes in the trailing 90 days; or the carrier is on the Elevated Risk list (see document 4 below).

4Repeat Pattern Rule

  • 3 or more flagged invoices from the same carrier — of any kind, detention or linehaul, not just the same charge type — within a rolling 90-day window requires Tier 2 review, regardless of any single invoice's dollar amount. A pattern of small discrepancies is itself a signal, even when no single invoice is large.

5What May Happen Without Extra Sign-off

  • May do directly: read invoice/shipment/dock data; calculate actual vs. billed detention; log a Tier 1 flag with its reasoning.
  • Always requires a second person's review, at any tier: disputing a charge with a carrier, holding or releasing a payment, or placing a carrier on the Elevated Risk list.

2. Linehaul Rate Verification Policy

Linehaul is not an accessorial charge — it's the base transportation rate itself, agreed at booking. Its check is simpler than detention's: no time dimension, only a dollar comparison.

2Linehaul Verification

  • A linehaul charge is a verified discrepancy when the billed amount exceeds the rate agreed at booking (in the TMS) by more than $25. As with detention, only an overcharge creates a dispute flag — billing less than agreed is a data-quality note, not a flag.

3Escalation — Same Rules as Detention

  • A verified linehaul discrepancy follows the exact same Escalation Tiers and Repeat Pattern Rule as detention (document 1, sections 3–4) — those rules key off the dollar amount and the carrier's overall flag history, not the charge type. A carrier's Tier 2 trigger counts linehaul and detention flags together: a pattern of billing problems is a pattern, regardless of which line item it shows up on.

3. Detention Dispute Workflow (SOP)

Document 1 defines the rule — what counts as a discrepancy, what tier applies. This defines the steps — what a person actually does once a flag exists.

Reviewing a Flagged Discrepancy (Analyst)

  • Open the flagged invoice and read the reconciliation facts (actual vs. billed detention) — confirm the numbers make sense; never approve or dismiss a flag based on its tier label alone.
  • If the discrepancy looks like a data problem rather than a billing problem, follow "Missing or Incomplete Dock Timestamps" below instead of treating it as a carrier dispute.
  • A Tier 1 flag needs no further analyst action once logged. A Tier 2 flag must be escalated to a manager — an analyst never resolves one directly.

Manager Review — Approving or Rejecting a Pending Flag

  • Review the flagged invoice, the reconciliation facts, and the carrier's full flag history.
  • Approve the flag if the discrepancy is confirmed and worth raising with the carrier — approving records that Northstar intends to dispute it, it does not dispute the charge itself.
  • Reject the flag if it turns out to be a data issue or a legitimate charge, and note why.
  • After the dispute conversation with the carrier concludes, record the outcome: Upheld if the carrier agreed the charge was wrong, or Denied if the carrier gave a legitimate explanation. Only an approved flag can later have an outcome recorded.

Missing or Incomplete Dock Timestamps

  • If a load's arrival or departure timestamp is missing, actual detention time cannot be computed and the invoice cannot be automatically flagged or cleared.
  • Do not assume a charge is legitimate just because it couldn't be checked — a missing timestamp is not evidence the charge is fine.
  • Check with dispatch or the carrier for the missing timestamp before approving payment; if it truly cannot be recovered, escalate to a manager rather than approving at face value.

Handling a Carrier on the Elevated Risk List

  • Every invoice from an Elevated Risk carrier is automatically Tier 2, regardless of dollar amount — a manager must personally review every one; none can be auto-approved.
  • Removing a carrier from the list is a manager-level decision, made only after a sustained period without new upheld disputes, and must be recorded with a reason.

4. Internal Memo — Carrier Risk Indicators

Confidential — Operations & Compliance. Helps distinguish an honest billing mistake from a pattern worth escalating: document 1 defines the dollar/time threshold for a single invoice; this memo defines when a carrier's behavior across multiple invoices should be treated as a risk signal.

Indicators of Elevated Carrier Risk

  • Repeated detention overbilling above the policy tolerance within a rolling 90-day window (the Repeat Pattern Rule, document 1 §4).
  • A load tendered to one carrier, then re-confirmed for pickup under a different MC number shortly before the scheduled pickup time — a classic double-brokering signal.
  • Invoice remittance (bank/payment) details changing between submissions without any prior notice from the carrier.
  • A carrier's safety rating dropping to "Conditional" at the same time as any open billing dispute.

Recommended Action

  • A carrier meeting two or more of the above indicators should be placed on the Elevated Risk list. Per document 1, any carrier on that list requires Tier 2 review for all future invoices, not only the ones individually flagged.
  • Placing a carrier on this list always requires human approval — it is never an automatic action.
