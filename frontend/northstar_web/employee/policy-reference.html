Accessorial Charge Audit & Dispute Policy

2Detention Charge Verification

  • A detention charge is a verified discrepancy only when the billed time differs from the actual dock dwell time by more than 15 minutes, and the resulting dollar difference is more than $25.
  • Only an overcharge (billed > actual) can be put on hold as a dispute. An undercharge is a data-quality note, never a hold.
  • Anything smaller than this is normal rounding/timestamp noise — do not hold it.

3Escalation Tiers

  • Tier 1 (any employee): a single invoice with a verified discrepancy under $250 can be resolved directly.
  • Tier 2 (Admin sign-off required): the discrepancy is $250 or more, OR the carrier already has 2+ upheld disputes in the trailing 90 days, OR the carrier is on the Elevated Risk list.

4Repeat Pattern Rule

  • 3 or more flagged/on-hold invoices from the same carrier within a rolling 90-day window requires Tier 2 review — regardless of any single invoice's dollar amount.

5What May Happen Without Extra Sign-off

  • Reading invoice/shipment/dock data, and holding a Tier 1 payment with a note, may be done directly.
  • Disputing a charge with a carrier, releasing a held payment, or placing a carrier on Elevated Risk always requires a second person's review.

Detention Dispute Workflow (SOP)

Reviewing a Flagged Discrepancy

  • Open the invoice and the real dock timestamps — confirm the numbers make sense before acting.
  • If the discrepancy looks like a data problem (e.g. a missing timestamp), don't treat it as a carrier dispute — see below.

Missing or Incomplete Dock Timestamps

  • Do not assume a charge is legitimate just because it couldn't be checked — that's not evidence the charge is fine.
  • Check with dispatch or the carrier for the missing timestamp before approving payment.

Carriers on the Elevated Risk List

  • Every invoice from an Elevated Risk carrier is treated as Tier 2, regardless of dollar amount — review it personally, never auto-approve.
